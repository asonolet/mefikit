Mesh overlay

Overlay operations combine two meshes by cutting one with the other’s edges. Mefikit exposes four families:

InputsMethodResult
2D + 2D, first orderoverlay(m2, op)UNION, INTERSECTION, DIFFERENCE, SYMMETRIC_DIFFERENCE or IMPRINT
2D + 2D, quadraticoverlay(m2, IMPRINT)subject refined by the cutter’s circular arcs
2D + 1Dintersect_1d(lines)subject refined by SEG2 / PLINE edges
2D surfaces in 3Doverlay_surfaces(m2)two mutually conformal surfaces

The 2D curved figures are drawn from the exact analytic arcs with Matplotlib; the boolean, 1D and 3D figures use PyVista. Plotting boilerplate and mesh builders are hidden. The visible cells contain only the operations: quad8 builds a single QUAD8 cell and mesh_area returns its exact area including the circular segments.

import numpy as np

import mefikit as mf

OP = mf.OverlayOperation

Boolean overlay (first-order meshes)

Two staggered QUAD4 grids are enough to show the five operations. IMPRINT keeps the subject’s domain and refines it with the other mesh’s edges; the four boolean operations select which pieces to keep.

OperationKeeps
UNIONeverything covered by at least one mesh
INTERSECTIONthe overlap
DIFFERENCEthe subject minus the other mesh
SYMMETRIC_DIFFERENCEwhat belongs to exactly one mesh
IMPRINTthe whole subject, refined

All five require first-order meshes (TRI3, QUAD4, PGON); passing a quadratic mesh to a boolean operation raises ValueError.

x = np.linspace(0.0, 3.0, 4)
mesh1 = mf.build_cmesh(x, x)
mesh2 = mf.build_cmesh(x + 1.25, x + 1.25)

results = {
    "Union": mesh1.overlay(mesh2, OP.UNION),
    "Intersection": mesh1.overlay(mesh2, OP.INTERSECTION),
    "Difference": mesh1.overlay(mesh2, OP.DIFFERENCE),
    "Symmetric difference": mesh1.overlay(mesh2, OP.SYMMETRIC_DIFFERENCE),
    "Imprint 2→1": mesh1.overlay(mesh2, OP.IMPRINT),
    "Imprint 1→2": mesh2.overlay(mesh1, OP.IMPRINT),
}

The ragged cell in the middle of Difference and Symmetric difference is a PyVista rendering artefact for non-convex polygons, not a mesh defect.

Quadratic edges are circular arcs

A quadratic 2D cell (TRI6, TRI7, QUAD8, QUAD9, QPOLY) stores its boundary as [vertices…, mid-side nodes…]: each edge is the circular arc through its two endpoints and its mid-side node. A collinear mid node degenerates to a straight segment.

IMPRINT accepts quadratic cells on both sides. It intersects arcs with segments or with other arcs and emits touched pieces as QPOLY whose mid-side nodes are recomputed on the original circles — so the exact curved area is preserved.

subject = quad8(0.20)
cutter = mf.build_cmesh([-0.25, 0.5, 1.25], [0.25, 0.75])
imprinted = subject.overlay(cutter, OP.IMPRINT)

before, after = mesh_area(subject), mesh_area(imprinted)
assert np.isclose(before, after, atol=1e-12)
print(f"exact curved area: {before:.9f} → {after:.9f}")
print("output types:", imprinted.block_types())

exact curved area: 1.550029316 → 1.550029316
output types: ['QPOLY']

Concave arcs

An edge may also bulge towards the interior of the cell. That arc sweeps clockwise instead of counter-clockwise, and the cut must still land on the right branch of the circle. Concave cells are supported too — including a cutter edge touching an arc tangentially: the resulting piece is then split at the pinch point rather than emitted as a self-touching polygon.

concave = quad8(0.25, inward=True)
concave_cut = concave.overlay(cutter, OP.IMPRINT)
assert np.isclose(mesh_area(concave), mesh_area(concave_cut), atol=1e-12)

Arc ↔ arc: a quadratic cutter

Two different circles meet through their radical axis; two arcs on the same circle share their overlap endpoints. The cutter’s curved edges refine the subject exactly where the arcs meet, and the new interface follows the cutter’s circular arc — not its chord. Outer arcs and the exact curved area are preserved.

other = quad8(0.10, shift=(0.35, 0.30))
arc_cut = subject.overlay(other, OP.IMPRINT)
assert np.isclose(mesh_area(subject), mesh_area(arc_cut), atol=1e-12)
print("refined pieces:", arc_cut.num_elements())

refined pieces: 2

Cutting a 2D mesh with a 1D mesh

intersect_1d is the 2D/1D counterpart of IMPRINT: the subject is refined with the edges of a 1D mesh of SEG2 or PLINE cells. A PLINE is split into its consecutive SEG2 edges, so both forms behave identically.

The 1D elements must not intersect one another. A closed polyline lying strictly inside a cell is ignored — the result would be a cell with a hole, which is not a valid element.

plane = mf.build_cmesh([0.0, 1.0], [0.0, 1.0])

segments = mf.UMesh(np.array([[-0.5, 0.5], [0.5, 0.5], [1.5, 0.5]]))
segments.add_regular_block("SEG2", np.array([[0, 1], [1, 2]], dtype=np.uintp))
cut_by_seg2 = plane.intersect_1d(segments)

zigzag = mf.UMesh(
    np.array([[-0.5, 0.3], [0.2, 0.3], [0.5, 0.7], [0.8, 0.3], [1.5, 0.3]])
)
zigzag.add_poly_block(
    "PLINE", np.arange(5, dtype=np.uintp), np.array([5], dtype=np.uintp)
)
cut_by_pline = plane.intersect_1d(zigzag)
print("SEG2 cutter:", cut_by_seg2.block_types(), cut_by_seg2.num_elements(), "cells")
print("PLINE cutter:", cut_by_pline.block_types(), cut_by_pline.num_elements(), "cells")

SEG2 cutter: ['PGON'] 2 cells
PLINE cutter: ['PGON'] 2 cells

Surfaces in 3D

overlay_surfaces imprints two 2D surfaces embedded in 3D wherever they coincide. Both must be piecewise planar. The refined surfaces share their intersection nodes, so they become mutually conformal on the overlap; parts not covered by the other surface are copied verbatim.

surface1 = quad_surface((0.0, 2.0, 0.0, 2.0), 4)
surface2 = quad_surface((1.0, 2.8, 0.5, 1.9), 5)
tilt = mf.Transform.rotation([1.0, 0.0, 0.0], -0.6) @ mf.Transform.rotation(
    [0.0, 1.0, 0.0], 0.5
)
surface1, surface2 = surface1.transform(tilt), surface2.transform(tilt)
surfaces = surface1.overlay_surfaces(surface2)

assert np.array_equal(
    np.asarray(surfaces.refined1.coords()), np.asarray(surfaces.refined2.coords())
)
print("input faces:", len(surfaces.parents1), "+", len(surfaces.parents2))

input faces: 16 + 25

Rules and current limits

  • Input meshes must be valid, planar and correctly oriented (CCW), with coordinates in the same space.
  • Boolean operations are first-order only. Their inside/outside classification assumes straight-edged cut pieces; passing TRI6, TRI7, QUAD8, QUAD9 or QPOLY raises ValueError. IMPRINT has no such restriction and is exact on curved cells.
  • A closed cutter loop strictly inside a cell is ignored (no holed cells).
  • 1D cutters are assumed not to intersect one another.
  • overlay_surfaces requires piecewise-planar first-order surfaces.
